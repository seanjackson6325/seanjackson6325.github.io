Thought

On Learning Physics vs. Doing Physics

There's a strange gap between understanding a derivation and having intuition for it. The harmonic oscillator makes perfect sense on paper — and yet every time a new system reduces to one, it still feels like a small miracle. I think that gap is where all the interesting stuff lives.

Interests

]
Science

Quantum Mechanics

There's something uniquely compelling about a theory that forces you to abandon classical intuition entirely. I spend a lot of time with the formalism — Hilbert spaces, operators, measurement — and even more thinking about what it actually means. Quantum information is a particular fixation right now: the idea that information is physical, and that entanglement is a resource, still feels like it shouldn't be true.

// ongoing
Research

ML Research

Outside of work projects, I try to stay current with the ML literature — particularly computer vision, geometric deep learning, and physics-informed neural networks. The intersection of physical priors and learned representations is where I think a lot of the most interesting open problems live right now.

// ongoing
Hobby

Physics Simulations

Writing n-body gravity sims, wave equation solvers, and chaotic pendulum integrators in Python whenever I want to see something click visually. There's something uniquely satisfying about watching an algorithm produce the same orbit Kepler described 400 years ago.

// ongoing
Science

Pure Mathematics

Taking a math minor changed how I think about everything. Real analysis, linear algebra, and abstract algebra have a kind of austere beauty that feels completely separate from their applications — even when those applications are everywhere. I read math textbooks recreationally, which I'm told is a personality flaw.
